# TVS Ronin 225: checked facts (TVS owner's manual + TVS spec sheets, Nov 2026)
# b: ronin

## TVS Ronin 225 specifications: engine, power, torque, weight, seat height
k: specs, cc, bhp, ps, nm, kerb weight, ground clearance, top speed, gears, dimensions
b: ronin
TVS Ronin 225: 225.9 cc single cylinder, 4-valve SOHC, oil-cooled, fuel injected. 20.4 PS (about 20.1 bhp) at 7,750 rpm and 19.93 Nm at 3,750 rpm. 5-speed gearbox with assist & slipper clutch. Bore × stroke 66 × 66 mm.
Kerb weight 159 kg (single-channel ABS) / 160 kg (dual-channel), seat 795 mm, ground clearance 181 mm, wheelbase 1,357 mm, tank 14 L. TVS quotes a top speed of 120 km/h in 5th. Idle (warm): about 1,300 rpm. Launched July 2022.

## Ronin tyre size and tyre pressure (TVS manual)
k: tyre pressure, psi, air pressure, tyre size, front tyre, rear tyre, 110/70, 130/70, pillion pressure, hawa
b: ronin
TVS Ronin tyres: front 110/70-17, rear 130/70-17, tubeless, on 17-inch alloys.
TVS manual pressures (cold): front 25 psi (solo and with pillion); rear 28 psi solo and 32 psi with a pillion/luggage.
Check pressures cold (before riding or after the bike has stood a while); a hot tyre reads 2–4 psi higher. Many RWB riders run a little more at the rear (30–33 psi) on highways or loaded; never go above what the tyre sidewall allows.

## Ronin engine oil grade and quantity
k: engine oil, oil capacity, how much oil, 10w30, oil grade, tru4, synthetic, jaso, 1.3 litre, oil change
b: ronin
TVS manual: TVS TRU4 Fully Synthetic SAE 10W-30, API SL, JASO MA2. Quantity at a regular oil change: 1,300 ml (1.3 L); after an engine overhaul 1,500 ml.
Change the oil and oil filter at every service (every 6,000 km or 6 months after the free services, whichever comes first), and clean the oil strainer. Any good 10W-30 (or 10W-40) fully synthetic with JASO MA2 works; JASO MA2 matters because the Ronin has a wet clutch. Check the level on the centre stand with a cool engine, dipstick wiped and rested on the threads unless the manual says screw it in.

## Ronin service schedule (TVS owner's manual)
k: service interval, free service, first service, second service, third service, maintenance chart, 6000 km
b: ronin
TVS Ronin service chart (whichever comes first):
- 1st free: 750–1,000 km or 30–45 days (oil + oil filter, strainer clean, tappet check)
- 2nd free: 5,500–6,000 km or 6 months
- 3rd free: 11,500–12,000 km or 12 months (also spark plug + air filter)
- Then every 6,000 km or 6 months: oil + oil filter, strainer clean, tappet check, chain clean/lube/adjust.
Spark plug and air filter: every 12,000 km (air filter sooner in dust). Brake fluid: check every service, replace every 21,000 km or 2 years. Front fork oil: 36,000 km, if needed. Free services cover labour; oil, filters and parts are paid.

## Ronin running-in (break-in) rules for a new bike
k: running in, break in, new bike, first 1000 km, new ronin tips
b: ronin
TVS manual: keep under 50 km/h for the first 1,000 km and keep varying the engine speed (no long constant speed, no full-throttle bursts). The 1st free service at 750–1,000 km (oil change included) is the most important one; don't skip it.

## Ronin chain slack, chain lube and chain care
k: chain slack, chain adjustment, chain tension, chain lube, chain cleaning, loose chain, tight chain, 20-25 mm
b: ronin
TVS manual: drive chain slack 20–25 mm (checked on the centre stand, in neutral, midway between the sprockets on the lower run). Lube about every 500 km (TVS suggests TRU SPRAY or TRU4 oil), and after riding in rain or washing. Clean it with a chain cleaner (not petrol/diesel) and a brush, let it dry, then lube the inside of the chain on a warm chain and wipe off extra after 10–15 minutes.
A too-tight chain makes a rattle/whine and wears sprockets and bearings fast; too loose can jump. Clean and adjust at every service.

## Ronin clutch free play and clutch cable
k: clutch play, clutch adjustment, hard clutch, clutch lever, clutch cable
b: ronin
TVS manual: clutch lever free play 8–13 mm at the lever tip. Too little play can make the clutch slip and hard shifts/false neutrals; too much makes neutral and 1st gear hard to find. Adjust at the lever's barrel adjuster, and keep the cable lubed. A frayed or sticky cable should be replaced.

## Ronin spark plug type and gap
k: spark plug, plug type, iridium, ngk, bosch, plug gap
b: ronin
TVS manual: Bosch UR4KE, gap 0.8–0.9 mm, replace every 12,000 km or 1 year. Several RWB riders use the NGK iridium CR7EIX as an upgrade. A worn or wet plug causes hard starting and misfires.

## Ronin battery, fuses and lights
k: battery, battery spec, 12v 6ah, fuse, fuse rating, headlight watt, abs fuse, ems fuse
b: ronin
TVS manual: battery 12 V 6 Ah VRLA (maintenance-free). Charge it with a proper charger if it reads below 12.4 V. Fuses (side panel fuse box): ABS 20 A (dual-channel) or 15 A (single-channel), DC load 15 A, EMS 10 A, plus a main fuse; spare fuses are in the box. All lights are 12 V LED (headlamp 30 W/12 W).

## Ronin brake fluid and brakes
k: brake fluid, brake oil, dot 4, dot 3, brake pads, disc size, abs
b: ronin
Ronin brakes: 300 mm front disc (2-piston caliper) and 240 mm rear disc, with ABS (single-channel on Base, dual-channel from Base+). Brake fluid: TVS Girling DOT 3 or DOT 4 (don't mix types). Replace every 21,000 km or 2 years; check the level and pad thickness at every service. RWB riders usually see front pads last about 8,000–15,000 km.

## Ronin fuel tank, fuel gauge bars and DTE (distance to empty)
k: fuel tank, reserve, fuel bars, low fuel light, dte, range, distance to empty, how far on reserve
b: ronin
Tank: 14 L (TVS spec). TVS manual fuel gauge: 9 bars above about 10.3 L, 4 bars at about 6.1 L, 1 bar at about 3.2 L; the low-fuel light blinks at about 2.6 L and stays on at about 2 L. DTE (Mode button cycles ODO, Trip A, Trip B, DTE) shows only with 1–8 bars. Don't switch the ignition on with an almost empty tank (it can harm the fuel pump). RWB riders report about 25–35 km after the low-fuel warning; refill early.

## Ronin real-world mileage
k: mileage, kmpl, fuel efficiency, average, claimed mileage, how much mileage
b: ronin
TVS claims about 42 km/l. RWB riders usually see high 30s to mid 40s (city about 37–43, highway 40–47 km/l), depending on riding style, tyre pressure, load and petrol. Smooth throttle, steady 60–75 km/h, correct tyre pressure and a clean, lubed chain help most. Log every fill in the app's Mileage screen to know your real figure.

## Ronin riding modes, ABS and traction control
k: rain mode, urban mode, ride mode, abs light, mode change error, ch err
b: ronin
The Ronin has Rain and Urban modes. Change modes only with the throttle closed. If the bike detects a fault in the engine management (EMS) or ABS, it runs only in Urban mode after an ignition reset and shows "MODE CHANGE ERROR" (or "Ch Err" on the non-connected cluster): get it checked. The Top variant adds traction control. An ABS warning light that stays on after you ride off means ABS isn't working: ride gently and get it checked.

## Ronin side stand sensor: bike won't start
k: side stand, won't start, no crank, kill switch, neutral, side stand indicator
b: ronin
The Ronin won't start with the side stand down (it shows a side-stand indication). If it won't crank: kill switch on RUN, side stand fully up, in neutral or clutch pulled, battery OK. RWB riders say the side-stand sensor can get dirty or weak with time (mud, grease); cleaning it often fixes it, otherwise replace it at the service centre.

## Ronin ISG / engine malfunction (MIL) warning light
k: isg light, check engine light, malfunction light, engine light, warning lamp, won't start isg, blue plug
b: ronin
The Ronin uses an ISG (Integrated Starter Generator: silent starts, no separate starter motor). If the ISG or engine malfunction light stays ON, get it checked at TVS. RWB riders report two common causes: the blue ISG connector under the right side of the tank working loose (unplug, clean, plug back firmly), and a damaged sensor/magnet coil wire near the front sprocket (often after the chain slaps it). If it feels unsafe, stop and call TVS roadside help.

## Ronin false neutral and hard gear shifts
k: false neutral, gear slipping, gear jumps, hard shift, neutral problem, gear sensor
b: ronin
Riders report occasional false neutrals on the Ronin. What helps: correct clutch free play (8–13 mm), a clean and lubed gear-shift linkage and pivot, a clean front sprocket area (lube gunk builds up there), correct chain slack, and a firm, full shift with your boot. If it keeps happening or the gear indicator acts up, ask TVS to check the gear position sensor (some riders had it replaced under warranty).

## Ronin rattling noises and vibration
k: rattle, noise, vibration, ticking, tick tick, sound, loose part
b: ronin
Common Ronin rattles reported by RWB riders: a loose number plate or its holder, a chain that's too tight or dry, and loose panel/visor screws. A light "tik-tik" from the engine when warm is normal on the Ronin. New or loud knocking, grinding or a change in sound means get it checked. Buzz through the pegs above about 80 km/h is a known single-cylinder trait.

## Ronin water in the instrument cluster or electricals after rain/washing
k: water in meter, fogged cluster, moisture console, washing damage, rain electrical
b: ronin
Avoid pressure-washing the cluster, switches, spark plug cap and connectors. If moisture fogs the cluster, park it in sun/shade with the ignition off and let it dry; if water stays or it glitches, show it at TVS (it may be a warranty item). After washing, dry with compressed air or a blower and wait until the engine is cool before washing (RWB washing guide).

## Ronin engine heating in traffic
k: overheating, engine heat, hot engine, heat on legs, traffic heat
b: ronin
The Ronin is oil-cooled (it has an oil cooler, no radiator or coolant). It gets warm in long traffic jams, which is normal; most RWB riders don't find it a problem. Keep the oil fresh and at the right level, don't idle for very long, and switch off at long signals if you're comfortable. Real overheating signs (oil warning, power loss, smell, smoke) mean stop and get it checked.

## Ronin oil leak or seepage
k: oil leak, oil seepage, drain bolt leak, oil mark, gasket
b: ronin
Small oil marks are most often from the drain bolt (replace its washer at every oil change and tighten correctly, don't over-tighten) or the oil filter cover O-ring. Seepage from the cylinder head points to a gasket. Wipe it clean, ride, and check where fresh oil appears, then show the service centre. A real leak (drips, oil on the tyre) means don't ride far: oil on a tyre is dangerous.

## Ronin bike pulls to one side / handlebar not straight
k: pulls left, pulls right, alignment, handle not straight, wheel alignment
b: ronin
Some Ronin owners report the bike pulling to one side. Check tyre pressures first, then rear wheel alignment (both adjuster marks equal), chain adjusters, steering head bearing and a bent fork after a fall. Ask the service centre to check alignment with a proper procedure.

## Ronin vs Hunter 350 vs XSR 155: quick comparison
k: compare, better bike, which to buy, ronin or hunter, ronin or xsr
b: ronin
Ronin 225: light (159–160 kg), easy, comfy upright scrambler-ish ride, USD fork, slipper clutch, good mileage (high 30s–40s), 5 speeds. RE Hunter 350: heavier (about 181 kg), 349 cc thump, more torque, bigger service network. Yamaha XSR 155: lightest (137 kg), revvy liquid-cooled VVA engine, stiffer suspension, small 10 L tank. Test ride all; for rough Bengal roads and touring comfort the Ronin is a sweet spot.

## Ronin tyre upgrades and wider rear tyre
k: tyre upgrade, better tyres, 140/70, wider tyre, best tyre for ronin, apollo tramplr, eurogrip, ceat, mrf
b: ronin
Stock: TVS Eurogrip block-tread tyres. Popular RWB upgrades: Apollo Tramplr XR (dual-purpose, good in rain and mud), TVS Eurogrip Protorq/Roadhound, MRF and CEAT road tyres. Some riders fit a 140/70-17 rear; it looks beefier and can feel planted, but check clearance with the chain/swingarm, and the speedometer may read slightly differently. Always use tubeless tyres of the right load/speed rating, and replace a tyre at the wear-bar or around 5–6 years old (check the DOT date).

## Ronin popular accessories and mods
k: accessories, mods, modification, crash guard, engine guard, saddle stay, top rack, visor, windscreen, mobile holder, usb charger

Popular with RWB riders: TVS official engine/leg guard (about ₹1,999) or Moto Torque crash guard, saddle stays and top rack for touring, tank bag (9–12 L), rackless saddle bags (22–46 L), TVS visor (looks more than wind protection), aux lights mounted low with the beam pointing down, a USB/Type-C charger (comes on newer variants), a navigation screen instead of a phone mount (phone mounts can damage phone camera OIS from vibration).

## Buying a used Ronin: what to check

Check: service history and bills, odometer vs wear (pegs, levers, tyres), chain/sprockets, fork seals for oil, brake pads/discs, cold start (should start instantly), warning lights all go off, no smoke, clean gear shifts, straight frame/steering after a test ride, RC/insurance/PUC and the VIN/engine numbers matching the RC, and no pending challans.
