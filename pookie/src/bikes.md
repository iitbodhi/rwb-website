## Yamaha XSR 155: specs, price, features
k: xsr155, xsr 155, yamaha xsr, retro yamaha, xsr specs, xsr price
b: xsr
Yamaha XSR 155 (launched in India Nov 2025, about ₹1.50–1.59 lakh ex-showroom): 155 cc liquid-cooled single with VVA, the same engine family as the R15 and MT-15 (India spec about 18.4 PS and 14.2 Nm, revs to 10,000 rpm), 6-speed with assist & slipper clutch. Inverted (USD) fork, link-type monoshock, dual-channel ABS, 282 mm front / 220 mm rear discs.
137 kg, 810 mm seat, 10 L tank. Tyres: front 100/80-17 (MRF Zapper FX1), rear 140/70-17 radial (MRF Revz-S). LCD with Y-Connect app (call/SMS alerts, last parked location). Battery 12 V 4 Ah.

## Yamaha XSR 155 maintenance: service interval, oil, tyre pressure
k: xsr service, xsr oil, xsr tyre pressure, xsr maintenance, yamalube
b: xsr
XSR 155 service interval is short: about every 4,000 km or 4 months (Team-BHP review), so keep to it. Yamaha's 155 cc engines use Yamalube 10W-40 (JASO MA); the sump is small, so check the oil level regularly and don't stretch oil changes. Liquid-cooled: keep coolant between the marks. Yamaha's 155 cc platform usually runs about 29 psi front / 33 psi rear (cold); confirm on the sticker on your bike.

## Yamaha XSR 155: pros, cons and common complaints
k: xsr problems, xsr issues, xsr cons, xsr review, xsr suspension stiff, xsr switchgear
b: xsr
Pros: gorgeous retro style, good fit and finish, rev-happy engine with good efficiency, roomy riding position, light and easy.
Cons owners mention: stiff R15/MT-15 suspension that's harsh on broken roads, budget-feeling switchgear with the horn and indicator buttons swapped (takes getting used to), short service interval, small 10 L tank, and not much low-end pull for a pillion and luggage on highways.

## Yamaha XSR 155 mods and accessories
k: xsr mods, xsr cafe racer kit, xsr scrambler kit, xsr accessories, xsr crash guard
b: xsr
Yamaha dealers offer a Cafe Racer kit (about ₹11,900) and a Scrambler kit (about ₹12,300), plus headlight guard, fly screen, bar-end mirrors, crash guards, radiator guard, adjustable levers and phone mounts (2025 prices). A radiator guard is worth it for broken roads. For touring add a tail bag and a tank bag, and plan fuel stops around the 10 L tank.

## Royal Enfield Himalayan 450: specs
k: himalayan 450, new himalayan, sherpa 450, himalayan specs, himalayan 452
b: himalayan450
RE Himalayan 450: 452 cc liquid-cooled DOHC "Sherpa" single, about 40 PS at 8,000 rpm and 40 Nm at 5,500 rpm, 6-speed with slip & assist clutch, ride-by-wire with Performance and Eco modes and switchable rear ABS. 17 L tank, about 196 kg kerb, seat 825–845 mm (low seat 805–825 mm), 21-inch front and 17-inch rear wheels: front 90/90-21, rear 140/80-17 (CEAT Gripp). ## Royal Enfield Himalayan 450 maintenance (RE owner's manual)
k: himalayan 450 oil, himalayan 450 tyre pressure, himalayan coolant, himalayan service, himalayan chain slack
b: himalayan450
RE manual (India): tyre pressure 32 psi front and rear, solo or with pillion. Engine oil 10W-40 API SN JASO MA2 semi-synthetic, about 2.1 L at an oil + filter change (check the sight glass; overfilling can affect the clutch). Coolant: ready-to-use RE-specified coolant (radiator about 815 ml at service + expansion tank to MAX). Brake fluid DOT 4 only. Spark plug gap 0.8–0.9 mm, battery 12 V 8 Ah. Chain free play 35–40 mm (long-travel suspension needs more slack). Clutch free play 9–11 mm. Running-in: under 6,000 rpm for the first 500 km. The service reminder asks for the 1st service early (about 450–500 km), then every 5,000 km.

## Royal Enfield Himalayan 450 known issues and tips
k: himalayan 450 problems, frame break, chassis crack, crash guard himalayan, himalayan issues
b: himalayan450
In 2024 a couple of Himalayan 450 frames broke; both had aftermarket crash guards fitted. The engine is a stressed part of the frame, so RE recommends its own (GMA) crash guards: use RE's guard or one designed to mount the same way. Owners have also reported the bike pulling to one side and early software/dash glitches fixed by updates. Fill only to the bottom of the anti-splash plate. Low-fuel warning comes at about 3 L.

## Royal Enfield Himalayan 411 (old Himalayan): basics
k: himalayan 411, old himalayan, bs4 himalayan, bs6 himalayan 411
b: himalayan411
The older Himalayan (411 cc air/oil-cooled, about 24.3 PS and 32 Nm, 5-speed) was replaced by the Himalayan 450 in late 2023. RE manual: tyres 90/90-21 and 120/90-17; pressure 25 psi front / 32 psi rear solo, 27 / 34 psi with pillion; engine oil 15W-50 semi-synthetic (JASO MA2); tank 15 L. ## Royal Enfield Classic 350 / J-series maintenance (RE manual)
k: classic 350 oil, classic 350 tyre pressure, hunter 350 service, re 350 oil change, 15w50, classic service interval
b: re350
RE Classic 350 (BS6 J-series) manual: tyre pressure 32 psi front and rear solo; with pillion 32 front, 36 rear. Engine oil 15W-50 API SL JASO MA2 semi-synthetic, about 1.7 L at an oil change. Services: 1st at about 500 km, 2nd at about 5,000 km, then every 5,000 km; the oil is replaced at the first service and then every 10,000 km (top up between; check level every 1,000 km). Chain free play 25–30 mm, clutch free play about 10–12 mm, spark plug gap 0.7–0.8 mm, battery 12 V 8 Ah, brake fluid DOT 4. Hunter and Meteor follow very similar schedules; check your sticker for their pressures.

## Royal Enfield 350 common issues and tips
k: classic 350 problems, hunter problems, meteor problems, re issues, rust chrome, heat right leg
b: re350
J-series 350s are refined and reliable. Owners mention: heat on the right leg in traffic, rust on chrome/exhaust if not cared for (rinse and protect after rain), side-stand switch and fuel-gauge glitches on some units, and vibration only at the top of the rev range. RE's network is huge, so parts are easy to get. Older (UCE) 350s need more frequent oil changes and tappet checks.

## Jawa and Yezdi bikes: models and engines

b: jawa
Jawa and Yezdi (Classic Legends) bikes use liquid-cooled DOHC singles with 6-speed gearboxes: about 294.7 cc (older Jawa 42, about 27 PS) and 334 cc (Jawa 350, 42 FJ, 42 Bobber, Perak, Yezdi Roadster/Scrambler/Adventure, about 29 PS). ## Jawa / Yezdi maintenance and common problems
k: jawa problems, jawa heating, jawa service, jawa oil, yezdi problems, jawa issues
b: jawa
Owners commonly report: heat on the legs in traffic (the radiator fan running is normal), early electrical/ECU glitches, weak batteries, chrome rust, and patchy service centres. Tips from owners: service on time (many change oil every 3,000–5,000 km), keep coolant at the mark, spray WD-40 on chrome monthly against rust, increase rear preload to stop scraping on speed breakers, and keep the chain lubed. Oil capacity is about 1.25 L on the 42 family (check the manual for grade).

## KTM 200 and 250 Duke, RC and 390 Adventure: basics and common issues
k: duke 200, duke 250, rc 200, rc 390, 390 adventure, ktm problems, ktm heating
b: ktm
Duke 200 (199.5 cc, about 25 PS), Duke 250 (about 31 PS) and 390 Duke share KTM's liquid-cooled, high-revving singles; RC models are faired, 390 Adventure (now X and R variants) is the touring/off-road one. ## Choosing a bike to buy (any brand)
k: which bike to buy, best bike, bike suggestion, first bike, budget bike, touring bike
b: any
Decide what you'll mostly do (city, highway touring, broken roads, off-road), your height (seat height and weight), budget including insurance and service, and the service network near you. Test ride your shortlist back to back, ride with a pillion if you'll carry one, and talk to owners (RWB has riders of many bikes: ask in the group). For Bengal roads and touring, a light bike with good suspension, a comfy seat and an easy service network matters more than peak power.
