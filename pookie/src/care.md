# General motorcycle care: true for any bike (b: any)

## Engine oil grades explained: 10W-30, 10W-40, 15W-50, synthetic, JASO MA2
k: oil grade meaning, viscosity, which oil, synthetic vs semi synthetic, mineral oil, jaso ma ma2, api sl sn
b: any
In "10W-30", 10W is how thin the oil is when cold (lower = flows faster at start-up) and 30 is its thickness when hot. Use the grade in YOUR bike's manual; a slightly thicker hot grade (e.g. 10W-40 instead of 10W-30) is a common choice in Indian heat, but don't go far off spec.
Fully synthetic lasts and protects best, semi-synthetic is in between, mineral is cheapest. For bikes with a wet clutch (almost all Indian bikes) the oil MUST say JASO MA or MA2; car oils and "energy conserving" oils can make the clutch slip. API SL/SN etc. is the quality level: use the manual's level or newer.

## How often to change engine oil
k: oil change interval, when to change oil, oil change km, oil life
b: any
Follow the manual's interval (it's usually given as km OR months, whichever comes first). Change sooner if you ride mostly in stop-go traffic, dust, heavy rain, very short trips, or flog it on highways in summer. Many riders in India change a bit earlier than the manual (e.g. at 4,000–5,500 km on 6,000 km intervals). Always change the oil filter with the oil, and replace the drain bolt washer.

## Checking engine oil level correctly
k: check oil, dipstick, sight glass, oil level, low oil
b: any
Bike upright (centre stand, or held straight if it only has a side stand), engine stopped for a few minutes. Dipstick bikes: wipe, insert as the manual says (many read with the dipstick resting, not screwed in), read between MIN and MAX. Sight-glass bikes: read the window with the bike upright. Don't overfill: too much oil can affect the clutch and seals.

## Tyre pressure basics: why it matters and how to check
k: tyre pressure tips, check air, cold pressure, nitrogen, inflate, psi, tyre pressure for highway
b: any
Check pressure at least weekly and before long rides, with the tyres COLD, using your own gauge (petrol pump gauges are often off). Use the figures on the sticker (chain guard/swingarm) or manual; add the pillion/load figure when riding two-up or with luggage.
Low pressure = heavy steering, heat, more wear and worse mileage; too high = less grip, harsher ride. Nitrogen is optional; the right pressure matters far more than the gas.

## Tubeless tyre puncture: how to fix on the road
k: puncture, flat tyre, tubeless repair, puncture kit, strip, inflator, nail in tyre
b: any
Carry a tubeless puncture kit (reamer + rubber strips + glue) and a small inflator. Steps: find the nail (soapy water shows bubbles), pull it out, ream the hole, push a glued strip in with the needle until about 1 cm sticks out, pull the needle out, trim, inflate to the right pressure, recheck after 10 minutes. It's a road fix: get it checked at a tyre shop. Don't repair sidewall cuts or big gashes; replace that tyre. Spoked rims with tubes need a tube or patch kit instead.

## When to replace tyres: tread depth, age, cracks
k: tyre life, worn tyre, tread, tyre age, dot code, tyre cracks, replace tyre
b: any
Replace a tyre when the tread reaches the wear bars (little bumps in the grooves, about 1 mm left), if it has cuts, bulges or deep cracks, or at about 5–6 years old even with tread left (rubber hardens). The DOT code's last 4 digits show week and year made (e.g. 2324 = week 23 of 2024). In monsoon, more tread = more safety. Change brake-hard rear tyres earlier.

## Chain cleaning and lubing (any bike)
k: chain lube, chain clean, chain maintenance, o-ring chain, x-ring, chain spray, wd40 chain
b: any
Lube every 500–600 km (more often in rain), clean every 1,000–1,500 km. Use a proper chain cleaner or kerosene sparingly, a soft brush, dry it, then spray lube on the INSIDE of the warm chain while turning the wheel (bike on a stand, engine OFF, never with the engine running in gear). Wipe excess after 10–15 min so it doesn't fling. O-ring/X-ring chains: avoid petrol, harsh solvents and pressure-washing directly. Check slack as per your manual.

## Chain and sprocket replacement signs
k: chain kit, sprocket worn, chain stretch, hooked teeth, chain noise
b: any
Replace chain and both sprockets together when: the chain keeps going slack soon after adjustment, the adjusters are near their limit, links are stiff/kinked, you can pull the chain off the rear sprocket by more than half a tooth, or sprocket teeth look hooked or pointed. Replacing only the chain on worn sprockets wears the new chain fast.

## Brake pads: how to check and when to replace
k: brake pad wear, brake squeal, spongy brake, pad thickness, sintered, organic
b: any
Look at the pads through the caliper: replace when about 1.5–2 mm of pad material is left, or when you hear grinding. Squeal often means dusty pads (clean with brake cleaner), glazed pads or a dry caliper pin. A spongy lever means air or old fluid in the lines: bleed and change the fluid. New pads need 100–200 km of gentle bedding in. Sintered pads last longer and work better wet; organic are gentler on discs.

## Brake fluid: DOT 3, DOT 4 and when to change
k: brake fluid change, dot 4, brake oil, spongy brakes, brake fluid colour
b: any
Brake fluid absorbs water over time, which lowers its boiling point (brakes can fade on long descents). Change it every 2 years (or as your manual says), regardless of km. Use only the DOT type written on the reservoir cap; never mix DOT 5 (silicone) with DOT 3/4. Dark fluid = old fluid. Brake fluid damages paint: wipe spills immediately.

## Battery care: weak battery, not starting, long parking
k: battery dead, battery drain, jump start, battery charger, voltage, bike not starting battery, battery life
b: any
A healthy 12 V bike battery reads about 12.6 V or more at rest; below about 12.4 V it needs charging. Most last 2–4 years. If you park for weeks, ride it weekly or use a trickle charger/maintainer. Clicking but no crank = weak battery or loose terminal (clean and tighten). Extra accessories (aux lights, chargers) should be wired through a relay and fuse so they don't drain it. Push/bump starting works on most manual bikes with a little charge (2nd gear, clutch in, run, release).

## Air filter: when to clean or replace
k: air filter, dusty ride, air cleaner, k&n, filter clean
b: any
Paper air filters are replaced at the manual's interval (often 10,000–12,000 km) and much sooner after dusty or off-road riding; you can tap/blow out light dust from the inside, but don't wash paper filters. Washable foam/cotton filters (like K&N) are cleaned and re-oiled. A clogged filter cuts power and mileage.

## Spark plug: signs of a bad plug
k: spark plug bad, misfire, hard start, plug colour, iridium plug
b: any
Signs: hard starting, misfire, jerks, poor mileage. A healthy plug tip is light tan/grey; black and sooty = rich/too much idling; oily = oil burning; white = lean/overheating. Use the exact plug type in your manual (heat range matters). Iridium plugs last longer but aren't magic. Replace at the manual's interval.

## Coolant for liquid-cooled bikes
k: coolant, radiator, overheating liquid cooled, coolant level, coolant change, fan
b: any
Liquid-cooled bikes (KTM Duke/RC, Himalayan 450, Guerrilla, Jawa/Yezdi, XSR 155, Apache RTR 310, Speed 400, Xpulse 210 etc.) need the coolant between MIN and MAX in the reservoir, checked with a cold engine. Use the coolant type in the manual (most are ready-mixed; don't top up with plain tap water except in an emergency). Replace as the manual says (often every 2 years or so). The radiator fan running in traffic is normal; temperature warning = stop and let it cool.

## Washing a motorcycle safely
k: washing bike, pressure wash, bike wash, cleaning, shampoo, polish
b: any
Wash with a cool engine (wait about 30 min after riding), use bike/car shampoo, and avoid pressure-washing directly on the cluster, switches, spark plug cap, connectors, wheel bearings, chain and air-intake. Dry with a blower or cloth (under the seat too), then lube the chain. Wax or a quick-detailer protects paint; a little WD-40 on bare metal and chrome (not brakes or tyres) slows rust.

## Rust prevention, especially in monsoon and near the sea
k: rust, corrosion, chrome rust, coastal ride digha, monsoon rust
b: any
After rain or sea-side rides (Digha, Mandarmani, Puri), rinse off salt and mud, dry fully, lube the chain and spray a light anti-rust/WD-40 on chrome, bolts and spokes (never on brakes, discs or tyres). Park under cover. Touch up paint chips early.

## Monsoon riding: bike prep and riding tips
k: rain riding, monsoon, wet roads, borsha, brishti, rain tips, waterlogged road
b: any
Prep: good tyres (enough tread), correct pressures, working lights and horn, chain lubed more often, brakes checked, a rain suit and an anti-fog visor or pinlock.
Riding: slow down and double your following distance; brake gently with both brakes, early and in a straight line; avoid painted lines, metal covers, oil patches and puddles hiding potholes; cross waterlogged stretches slowly in 1st/2nd at steady throttle, never deeper than the exhaust or air intake. Use the bike's rain mode if it has one. After deep water, dry the brakes by gentle braking.

## Riding through water: engine stopped in water (hydrolock)
k: water logging, flood, engine stopped water, hydrolock, water in engine
b: any
If the engine dies in deep water, DON'T try to restart it: water may have entered the engine through the air intake or exhaust, and cranking can bend parts (hydrolock). Push it out, call the service centre or roadside assistance. Milky engine oil = water inside: don't ride. Insurance "engine protect" add-ons cover this kind of damage.

## Long ride / touring checklist (pre-ride checks)
k: pre ride check, before long ride, touring checklist, trip preparation, inspection
b: any
Before a long ride: tyre pressure and condition, chain slack and lube, brake pads and fluid, engine oil level, lights/horn/indicators, clutch and throttle cables, loose bolts (number plate, mirrors, crash guard), battery, papers (DL, RC, insurance, PUC) and fuel. Carry: puncture kit + inflator, basic tools, fuses, spare clutch/throttle cable on far trips, zip ties, tape, chain lube, first aid, rain gear and power bank. Share your ride on RWB Track.

## Toolkit to carry on rides
k: toolkit, tools, what to carry, spare parts trip, emergency kit
b: any
Basic kit: tubeless puncture kit, mini inflator, combination spanners/sockets for your bike's sizes, Allen keys, screwdrivers, pliers, spare fuses, zip ties, insulation tape, a little chain lube, rag, torch. On far trips add: clutch and throttle cables, spark plug and plug spanner, brake pads, bulbs/relays if your bike uses them, tow rope and a jump starter. RWB's "Parts to carry during long rides" guide in the app (Guides → Maintenance) has a full list.

## Fuel: normal vs premium petrol, E20 ethanol petrol
k: premium petrol, power petrol, speed petrol, xp95, octane, e20, ethanol, fuel quality, mileage drop
b: any
Since April 2025, E20 petrol (20% ethanol) is the normal petrol at Indian pumps. Bikes made from about April 2023 are built for it (Royal Enfield says its BS6 bikes are E20-compliant since 2020; Bajaj says its bikes from the last 10 years are, including KTM). Government/ARAI tests say mileage can drop about 2–6%; some riders report more. Older bikes may need rubber fuel parts changed over time.
Premium (higher-octane) petrol only helps engines designed for it (high compression); for most commuter and 200–400 cc bikes, normal petrol of the right type is fine. Fill from busy, reputed pumps; you can ask to see the density check.

## Fuel additives: are they good?
k: fuel additive, octane booster, injector cleaner, o2 sensor
b: any
Mostly not needed with good petrol. If you use one, pick a quality one labelled "oxygen-sensor safe" (PEA-based) and don't overuse it: cheap additives with lead, silicone or manganese can damage the O2 sensor. RWB's guide suggests at most about every 3,000 km (or every 5–6 full tanks).

## Improving mileage on any bike
k: better mileage, save fuel, fuel economy tips, increase kmpl
b: any
Smooth throttle and early upshifts, steady 50–70 km/h in top gear where possible, correct tyre pressure, clean and lubed chain with the right slack, fresh oil, clean air filter and good plug, less idling, less weight and no dragging brakes. Track every fill in the RWB app's Mileage screen to see what helps.

## Riding a new bike: break-in for any motorcycle
k: running in, break in, new bike first service
b: any
Follow your manual's running-in rules (usually low revs/speed for the first 500–1,000 km, varying speed, no full throttle, no long idling) and do the first service on time: it removes early metal particles with the first oil change and re-tightens everything.

## Clutch slipping, hard clutch, or bike creeping in neutral
k: clutch slip, clutch hard, clutch drag, creeping, clutch plates
b: any
Slipping (revs rise, bike doesn't speed up) = too little free play, worn plates, or wrong oil (non-JASO MA). Dragging (bike creeps with clutch pulled, hard to find neutral) = too much free play, old oil or cable issues. Set the free play to the manual figure first, use the right oil, then check plates.

## Engine overheating warning signs
k: overheat, temperature light, boiling, coolant warning, engine too hot, smell
b: any
Stop safely if you see the temperature/oil warning, smell burning oil, see steam/smoke, or power drops. Let it cool with the engine off (don't open a hot radiator cap). Check oil (and coolant on liquid-cooled bikes), fan working, radiator not blocked by mud, and chain/brakes not dragging. Don't keep riding an overheating engine.

## Warning lights on the dashboard: what to do
k: warning light, engine light, mil, abs light, oil light, battery light, check engine
b: any
Engine/MIL light staying on = fault stored: ride gently to a service centre and get it scanned. Oil pressure light = stop at once and check oil. ABS light staying on after moving = ABS not working: brake gently. Battery/charging light = charging problem: switch off accessories and get it checked. Temperature light = stop and cool down. Lights that blink briefly at start-up are just self-checks.

## Storing a bike for weeks (parking long time)
k: long parking, storage, not riding for month, bike stored
b: any
Fill the tank (less condensation), clean and lube the chain, inflate tyres a bit above normal or use a stand, disconnect the battery or keep it on a maintainer, cover the bike, and plug the exhaust against insects. Before riding again: check tyres, brakes, battery, oil and that nothing nested in the air box.

## Service centre tips: avoid overcharging
k: service centre tips, overcharged, job card, unnecessary parts, service bill
b: any
Write down your issues and give them on the job card, ask for an estimate before work, ask them to call before replacing anything extra, ask for old parts back, check the bill line by line, and keep every bill (log it in the RWB app's Service screen). Buy consumables like chain lube yourself if they charge too much. Be polite but firm about anything not in the manual's schedule.

## Insurance for motorcycles: add-ons worth taking
k: insurance renewal, zero depreciation, add ons, comprehensive insurance, third party, claim
b: any
Third-party insurance is the legal minimum; comprehensive (own damage) covers your bike. RWB's insurance guide recommends these add-ons: zero/nil depreciation, return to invoice, rim and tyre protection, and engine and gearbox protection (water/flood damage). Roadside assistance and personal accident cover are also useful. Renew before expiry to keep your no-claim bonus.
