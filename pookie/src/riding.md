# Riding, gear, touring and road rules (b: any)

## Group ride rules and etiquette (RWB rides)
k: group ride, first ride with rwb, formation, lead, sweep, marshal, staggered, ride rules
b: any
On RWB group rides: reach the meeting point on time with a full tank, follow the ride lead and never overtake them, the sweep (last rider) stays at the back so nobody gets left behind, marshals guide at turns. Ride in a staggered line on highways with a safe gap, single file on narrow or twisty roads. Keep your place in the line, signal early, no stunts or racing, keep the rider behind in your mirror at turns. Share your location on RWB Track (pick the event) so the team can see everyone.

## Riding gear: helmet standards (ISI, DOT, ECE) and what to buy
k: helmet, isi, bis, ece, dot, full face, helmet brand, riding gear, budget gear
b: any
In India a BIS (ISI-marked) helmet is the legal minimum; ECE 22.06 or DOT certification on top of ISI is a good sign of quality. Choose a full-face helmet that fits snugly (no wobble, cheeks pressed), with a clear visor and pinlock for rain. Then a CE-level jacket with armour (L1/L2), gloves, riding pants with knee armour and over-ankle boots. RWB riders' rough minimum budget: helmet around ₹5k, jacket around ₹6k, plus gloves, pants and boots.

## Night riding on highways
k: night riding, dark highway, headlight, fog light, dipper, trucks at night
b: any
Ride slower than by day, use high beam only when nothing is coming and dip early for oncoming traffic, wear reflective gear, keep a clean visor (scratched visors flare), watch for unlit trucks, tractors, animals and potholes, take breaks every 1–1.5 hours, have tea/coffee, and avoid heavy meals before riding. Aux lights must point down so they don't blind others.

## Riding with a pillion
k: pillion, passenger, riding two up, pillion tips, back seat
b: any
Raise the rear tyre pressure (and rear preload if adjustable) as the manual says for a pillion. Ask the pillion to sit close, hold your waist or the grab rail, keep feet on the pegs, lean with the bike and not move suddenly. Brake earlier and smoother, accelerate gently, and go slower over bad roads. The pillion must also wear a helmet (it's the law).

## Braking correctly and ABS
k: how to brake, front brake, rear brake, emergency braking, skid, abs tips
b: any
The front brake does most of the stopping (about 70%). Use both brakes together, squeeze progressively (don't grab), and brake mostly in a straight line before a turn. With ABS, in an emergency squeeze hard and keep it on; ABS stops the wheels locking. Without ABS, ease off if a wheel locks and reapply. On wet or gravel roads, be gentler and leave more distance.

## Cornering basics
k: cornering, turning, bends, ghats, hairpin, lean, counter steering
b: any
Slow down before the bend (finish braking while upright), look through the turn to where you want to go, keep a steady or slightly rising throttle through it, and lean with the bike. A light push on the inside handlebar makes the bike lean (counter-steering). On ghats and hairpins use a lower gear, stay in your lane, and avoid gravel and wet leaves at the apex.

## Riding in hills: Darjeeling, Sikkim, ghats
k: hill riding, mountain, uphill, downhill, ghat road, engine braking, sikkim, darjeeling
b: any
Uphill: lower gear early, steady throttle, don't ride the clutch. Downhill: use engine braking (stay in a low gear) and brake gently in short bursts so the brakes don't overheat. Honk at blind corners, stay left, give way to uphill traffic on narrow roads and expect landslide debris, wet patches and fog. Check brakes and pads before a hill trip.

## High altitude riding: Ladakh, Spiti, North Sikkim
k: high altitude, ladakh, spiti, nathu la, ams, mountain sickness, oxygen, cold start altitude
b: any
Bikes lose power at altitude (thinner air) and fuel-injected bikes adjust automatically. Cold mornings can make starting slower: let the battery recover between cranks. For you: acclimatise slowly, drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol, and descend if you get bad headache, breathlessness or confusion (AMS). Many border/protected areas need permits or fees (e.g. parts of Sikkim and Ladakh): check the official government portals before you go, carry ID copies, and fill fuel whenever you can.

## Highway cruising and long-distance comfort
k: highway, cruising speed, long ride comfort, saddle sore, fatigue, ride all day
b: any
Cruise where the engine feels relaxed (for small singles that's usually 70–90 km/h), take a 10–15 min break every 1–1.5 hours, stay hydrated, wear earplugs against wind noise, and stand on the pegs briefly to rest your back. A gel/air seat cushion helps on long days. Stop riding when tired: fatigue causes more crashes than speed.

## Accident: what to do (first steps)
k: accident, crash, fell, injury, someone hurt, first aid, emergency number
b: any
Make the spot safe (hazard lights, warn traffic), call 112 for police/ambulance if anyone is hurt, don't remove an injured rider's helmet unless they can't breathe, don't move them unless there's danger. Send SOS in the RWB app so the RWB team gets your location. Take photos, note vehicle numbers and witness contacts, and inform your insurer quickly. Good Samaritans helping accident victims are legally protected in India.

## Traffic rules and fines: documents to carry
k: documents to carry, dl rc puc insurance, digilocker, mparivahan, traffic police, challan
b: any
Carry (or keep in DigiLocker/mParivahan, which are legally valid): driving licence, RC, valid insurance and PUC. Both rider and pillion must wear helmets. Check pending challans on the official Parivahan e-challan site. Keep copies of your documents in the RWB app's Documents screen.

## Are loud exhausts, aux lights and fancy number plates legal?
k: legal mods, illegal modification, loud exhaust, silencer, aux light legal, fancy number plate, hsrp, modified bike
b: any
Loud aftermarket exhausts and removing the catalytic converter or silencer are illegal (noise and emission rules) and can be fined or the bike seized. Number plates must be the standard HSRP; fancy fonts are fined. Aux/fog lights are a grey area: keep them low, aimed down, not blinding, and switch them off in the city. RWB doesn't support illegal mods.

## PUC and insurance renewal
k: puc renewal, pollution certificate, insurance expiry, renew online
b: any
Renew PUC and insurance before they expire (riding without them can be fined). PUC is done at any authorised pollution testing centre (many petrol pumps); insurance can be renewed online by comparing quotes. Add the new expiry dates in the RWB app (Home → Documents) and it will remind you next time.

## Rain gear and staying dry
k: rain suit, raincoat, waterproof, rain cover, waterproof boots, gloves rain
b: any
A two-piece rain suit over your riding gear, waterproof gloves (or a spare dry pair), shoe covers or waterproof boots, a rain cover for the tank/tail bag, zip-lock bags for phone and papers, and an anti-fog visor (pinlock) or anti-fog spray. Bright or reflective rain gear helps others see you.

## Phone mount vs navigation screen
k: mobile holder, phone mount, camera damage, ois, navigation, carplay, android auto
b: any
Phone mounts are cheap and handy, but engine vibration can damage phone camera stabilisation (OIS) even with dampers, and phones overheat in the sun. A dedicated bike navigation screen (Android Auto/CarPlay) is safer for the phone and easier to see, but costs more and needs proper wiring. RWB's guide "Mobile Phone Holders or Navigation Screens" in Guides compares them.

## Intercoms for riding
k: intercom, bluetooth intercom, communication device, bluarmor, helmet communication
b: any
RWB primarily prefers Bluarmor intercoms for group rides. Look at range, battery life, rain resistance and whether it pairs with your friends' units. Keep volume reasonable so you still hear traffic.
